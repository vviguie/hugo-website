abstract: Automobility's global role in providing mobility services but with adverse
  impacts on climate change and land use makes it a critical topic at a planetary
  scale. So far the role of cars has been mostly addressed from a techno-economic
  perspective, with complementary but fragmented insights from other social sciences.
  We provide evidence that understanding automobility as a sub-system of the technosphere
  compromising human-made artifacts and structures with impacts at planetary scale
  enables investigations from a systems perspective. Our empirically grounded characterization
  of causal loops provides insights on intervention points guiding co-evolutionary
  trajectories between automobility, societies, and local and planetary dynamics along
  different temporal and spatial scales. We systematically draw on existing modeling
  approaches for outlining how to model automobility as technosphere in a reduced
  form. We conclude that policies, measures and cultural shifts should be systematically
  evaluated according to their impact on car-dependent systems. Our approach offers
  novel insights for reorienting automobility toward pathways consistent with low
  planetary impact and high well-being.
