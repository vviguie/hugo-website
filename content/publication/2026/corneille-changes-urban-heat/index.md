abstract: The number of elderly people potentially exposed to heat is an important
  metric for public health policies. This metric is expected to substantially increase
  in many developed countries due to the combination of climate change and population
  aging. However, existing assessments typically estimate future exposure by combining
  projected temperatures and demographic changes at coarse spatial scales, implicitly
  assuming that temperature and population are uniformly distributed within urban
  areas. Here, we quantify the bias introduced by this assumption by combining high-resolution
  climate simulations with fine-scale demographic data for the Paris metropolitan
  area (France). We assess, for the years between 2017 and 2022, to what extent accounting
  for the spatial heterogeneous changes in both urban temperatures and population
  distributions matters for assessing heat exposure of elderly people. We find that
  not taking into account these fine-scale dynamics systematically modify, by 22 to
  48%, elderly people's exposure to tropical nights. The evaluation bias is also widely
  variable from one year to another, as the urban heat island effect has different
  amplitude and extent depending on complex weather conditions. Our results demonstrate
  that future urban heat exposure depends not only on climate change and demographic
  growth but also on fine scale heterogeneities in the urban climate and in where
  older people are living within cities. These findings highlight the importance of
  integrating high-resolution climate and demographic information into large-scale
  heat exposure assessments, and the role that changes in intra-urban population distribution
  can play in adaptation strategies.
